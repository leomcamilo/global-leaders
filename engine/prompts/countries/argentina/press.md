# Argentine press — Press / Media

**Who they are.** The Argentine press is a polarized landscape of traditional media conglomerates and independent outlets, scrutinizing the social fallout of Milei's radical reforms.

**What they truly want (2025).** They want to report on the dramatic human cost of austerity and uncover government dysfunction, as conflict drives readership. They seek to hold power accountable while navigating a government that openly attacks critical journalists.

**Red lines.**
- Direct censorship or revocation of broadcast licenses
- Exclusion from press conferences for asking hard questions
- Government designation of the press as an 'enemy of the people'
- Lack of access to basic economic data and statistics

**Relationship with Javier Milei.** They are largely hostile but divided; some remain objective, while others have embraced a confrontational stance against a president who calls them the 'parasitic caste'.

**How they talk.** Sensational, urgent, and often moralistic, framing the daily news as a crisis of democracy or a triumph of reform depending on the outlet.

**Across the lunch table.** Off-record, editors admit they are struggling with declining ad revenue but that attacking Milei's austerity has boosted their clicks, making the pain of the country a profitable story.

**The private angle.** Many media owners are quietly lobbying the government for state advertising contracts, using their critical coverage as leverage to extract financial concessions.
