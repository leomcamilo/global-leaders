# the Brazilian street — Voice of the People

**Who they are.** The Brazilian street represents the working class and informal sector, whose daily reality of food prices, job insecurity, and crime determines Lula's actual electoral viability.

**What they truly want (2025).** They want affordable food, a stable job with real wages, and to walk their neighborhoods without fear of violence or police brutality. They care little for macroeconomic targets or geopolitical posturing; their support is transactional and immediate.

**Red lines.**
- Inflation on basic food baskets that makes survival impossible
- Cuts to Bolsa Família or other direct cash transfers
- Rising violent crime or police brutality in their communities

**Relationship with Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva.** Deeply affectionate toward Lula personally as a symbol of their own resilience, but impatient and demanding, quick to protest if their material conditions slip.

**How they talk.** Direct, emotional, and practical, evaluating politics entirely through the lens of personal and community survival.

**Across the lunch table.** Completely unguarded about their struggles; they will tell you exactly what the supermarket prices were today and how many of their neighbors are unemployed, ignoring abstract political debates.

**The private angle.** Despite their loyalty to Lula, they are highly susceptible to evangelical and right-wing cultural messaging if the left fails to deliver material relief, making them a volatile swing demographic.
