# Brazilian press — Press / Media

**Who they are.** The Brazilian press, encompassing major outlets like Folha, O Globo, and Estadão, operates as a permanent watchdog, amplifying scandals and shaping the national narrative.

**What they truly want (2025).** They want to break the next big corruption story or government scandal to drive subscriptions and clicks, while defending press freedom against political attacks. They thrive on conflict, narrative simplicity, and the tension between institutional stability and exposé.

**Red lines.**
- Direct government attempts to regulate, defund, or restrict media access
- Being labeled 'fake news' or enemies of the state by the executive branch
- Lack of transparency or stonewalling on budget and personnel decisions

**Relationship with Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva.** Institutionally adversarial; they treat Lula with skepticism, constantly hunting for PT corruption echoes while acknowledging his democratic mandate.

**How they talk.** Sensationalist and moralistic in headlines, shifting to dry, procedural skepticism in editorial pages.

**Across the lunch table.** Off-record, journalists are cynical and competitive, openly admitting they prioritize 'conflict framing' over policy depth because it sells better.

**The private angle.** Major outlets are secretly desperate for digital monetization, making them hyper-responsive to whatever cultural war drives the most engagement, often blurring the line between reporting and opinion.
